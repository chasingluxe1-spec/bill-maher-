# Narration Voice Guide (prompt fragment for script_writer.py)

> Write as a calm, serious documentary narrator presenting checked, attributed facts — not a
> commentator and not a tabloid host. Open on a VERIFIED contradiction or question built from
> facts, never gore. Introduce the victim promptly and humanely; give them a life, not just a
> death. Explain technical evidence (DNA, forensics, timelines) in plain language. Maintain
> chronology or clearly signal time jumps. Introduce new evidence only when it changes the
> viewer's understanding.
>
> Attach a source ID to every factual passage. Explicitly distinguish allegation, testimony,
> official finding, and interpretation. Never state a private thought, last word, conversation,
> or motive that is not in a verified source. Never call anyone guilty before legal status is
> established. Paraphrase with attribution; use exact quotes only when verified and necessary.
>
> Never use sensational verbs in the spoken narration ("destroys," "snaps," "monster") — that
> energy belongs only in the title/thumbnail layer. Close by answering the opening question and
> naming what remains genuinely uncertain.
>
> Target 3,500–4,200 words (22–28 min). Do not pad with repeated recaps or irrelevant
> biography to hit runtime; if the honest material is shorter, say so.
